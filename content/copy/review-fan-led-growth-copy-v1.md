# Copy review: /fan-led-growth ("Why fans")

**Written:** 22 Jul 2026
**Reviewing:** the page as it stands after `copy-reduction-inner-pages-v1.md`
**Scope:** copy only. Design review is the next pass, separately.

Read in reading order, as a stranger arriving from a search result for
"how to turn customers into fans". That is the page's job per
`seo-audit-nav-and-targeting-22jul.md`, so it is the lens used throughout.

**Verdict:** the argument is sound and it lands in the right order. What it is,
what the evidence says, why fans behave that way, what to do about it, who it
is for, where to start. Nothing needs restructuring. There is one factual bug,
one term used two ways, one section that says the same thing twice, and a close
that offers four exits.

## 1. "All four" has no fourth. Factual bug.

**Section:** What to do about it.

> Most companies already believe it. What stops them is that no single team owns
> it: **brand, product and community** each hold a piece, and nobody holds the
> whole. [...] The Fan Engine™ runs **all four** as one system.

Three are named. "All four" then asks the reader to count something that isn't
there. Growth is the missing discipline, and this page never names it, so a
reader who has not been to `/fan-engine` cannot resolve the reference. The hero
close has the same three-item list ("your brand, your product, and your
community pulling the same way").

**Recommended:** name growth in the first paragraph, where the list is doing
real work.

> ...no single team owns it: **brand, product, community and growth** each hold
> a piece, and nobody holds the whole.

One word, and "all four" lands. Leave the hero close at three: it is about who
makes a fan, and growth is the outcome rather than a maker.

| Option | Fix | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | Add "and growth" to the first list | Keeps the Fan Engine's four, costs one word |
| B | Change "all four" to "all of it" | Smallest edit, but loses the structure the Engine sells |
| C | Add a fourth to both lists | Most consistent, but the hero close gets baggy |

## 2. "Superfan" and "top fans" are the same person, one line apart

**Section:** The evidence.

> Not every customer becomes a **superfan**. The ones who do behave differently.
> → 2 to 3× longer **top fans** stay · 66 to 80% more **top fans** spend

Two words for one idea, in adjacent lines. Normally: pick one. Here there is a
reason not to. "Superfans" is a real search term with published demand behind
it, and the meta description already carries it. The stat labels are shared
word for word with `/fan-score` and must not drift.

**Recommended:** keep both, but bridge them so the reader knows they are the
same person.

> Not every customer becomes a superfan. **Your top fans** behave differently,
> and it shows up in the numbers.

Keeps the search term, makes "top fans" in the labels resolve, and is a word
shorter.

## 3. The evidence band and the first three cards say the same three things

This is the one worth real thought.

| The evidence (stats) | Why fans (cards 1 to 3) |
|---|---|
| 2 to 3× longer top fans **stay** | **They invest more**: "Fans stay longer and spend more" |
| 66 to 80% more top fans **spend** | (same card) |
| ~4× more often top fans **refer** | **They recommend you**: "Fans bring their friends in" |

Stay, spend, refer, with numbers. Then stay, spend, refer, without numbers,
about a screen later. Cards 4, 5 and 6 (they defend you, AI recommends you too,
it compounds) are all new information and earn their place.

**Recommended:** cut card 1 ("They invest more") and card 3 ("They recommend
you"), keep "They spread the word", and let the grid run at four. The evidence
band already made those two points, harder, with sources on them.

That is ~26 words and turns a 6-card grid into a 4-card grid, which also helps
the density complaint. It is the only cut on this page I would still make, and
it is a judgment call rather than a defect, so it is not applied.

**Against doing it:** the six cards are the most scannable answer to the query
this page targets, and a reader who skipped the stats loses two reasons. If
that worries you, keep six and instead retitle card 1.

**Separately, card 1's title does not match its copy.** "They invest more" over
"Fans stay longer and spend more" is the only place the page says "invest".
Fans spend; investors invest. "They stay and spend more" is plainer and true to
the sentence under it.

## 4. The close asks for four things

**Section:** Where to start.

> "the next step is a number, not a meeting."
> → See what your fans are worth · See how we'd work together
> → Not ready for either? Take the 2-minute Fan Score
> → Or tell me about your brand.

Four exits in one band, and the fourth is a meeting, which the line above just
said this was not. The tiering is deliberate and the "Or" softens it, but a
reader who has read 700 words and agreed with you now has a four-way decision.

Also: **"See what your fans are worth" is the same label as the Fan Value CTA
in the middle of the page**, pointing at the same place. That repetition is
correct (same label, same destination) and worth keeping.

**Recommended:** drop "Or tell me about your brand" from this page. Contact is
in the nav as "Get in touch" on every screen, and the close is stronger at
three. If a route to a conversation must stay here, it should be the second
button ("See how we'd work together" already goes to `/services`, which opens
with two ways to talk).

## 5. Smaller notes, no action needed

- **The own/rent motif fires four times** (hero lede, pull card, hero close,
  closing H2). That is the page's spine and the repetition is doing work. The
  one spot to watch is the pull card: "Growth you own, not rent" lands about
  forty words after the lede that said the same thing in the same words, so it
  reads as emphasis rather than a new beat. Fine as a design device, which is
  where it belongs in the next pass.
- **"And here is what it looked like when I built it →"** is a long link label
  starting with "And". It works in the sentence, but as a link target it is
  vague about where it goes. "See what it looked like when I built it →" reads
  the same and starts with the verb.
- **"It compounds"** says "the engine keeps working" before the Fan Engine has
  been named on this page (the hero close says "an engine you own"). It reads
  fine, and naming the Engine earlier would pull the trademark forward of the
  argument, which is the thing the SEO work deliberately moved back.
- **Voice rules pass.** No em dashes, no compare-down framing, no figure
  labels, Fan Engine™ / Fan Score / Fan Value used canonically throughout.
- **Every number is sourced and unchanged.** 2 to 3×, 66 to 80%, ~4×, Bain,
  Nielsen, HBR, Wharton, and the $560K / $5M example with its "not your
  numbers" caveat intact.

### Why the card cut is a design decision, not a copy one

The grid is `repeat(3, minmax(0,1fr))`. Six cards fill two rows exactly.
Cutting two leaves four: a row of three and one card alone on the second row,
which is worse to look at than the redundancy it fixes. The real options are
all layout calls:

1. **Keep six.** The redundancy stays. Cheapest, and the retitle already
   removes the worst of the overlap.
2. **Cut to three, one clean row.** Keeps "they spread the word", "they defend
   you" and "AI recommends you too", loses "it compounds" and the referral
   card. Saves ~40 words and a whole row of height. The most aggressive.
3. **Cut to four and run the grid at two columns**, matching the situations
   grid lower down. Fixes the orphan, but puts two identical two-column grids
   on one page, which is the card-grid monotony the homepage work diagnosed.
4. **Shrink the evidence band instead.** If the stats became a single line
   rather than a full band, the cards would stop repeating something the reader
   just spent a screen on. This is the option that solves the cause.

My call is 4, then 1. Worth deciding in the design pass with the page in front
of us rather than on paper.
