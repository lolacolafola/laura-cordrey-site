# Copy review: /services ("Work with me")

**Written:** 22 Jul 2026
**Reviewing:** the page after `copy-reduction-inner-pages-v1.md` (1273 → 1026 words)
**Scope:** copy only. Design pass is next, separately.

Read in reading order, as a buyer who already knows who Laura is. That is the
right lens: this page is commercial, not informational, and per the SEO
handover its readers arrive knowing the name or via an internal link.

**Verdict:** the structure is the best thing on the site. Five offers, each with
the same four beats (Need it when / What you get / Payoff / Proof) plus a price
and duration line, and a six-row index above them that works as a real skim
layer. It is genuinely easy to buy from.

The problems are all small and all consistency: one repeated sentence, one
offer missing a beat the others have, one idea called two different things, and
one claim the page contradicts elsewhere.

---

## 1. The H1 and the index heading are the same sentence

> **H1:** "The whole engine, or just the piece you need."
> **Index h2, one screen later:** "Start with the whole engine, or one piece."

This is partly my doing. The reduction pass cut the 46-word lede that used to
sit under the index heading, which was the right call, but that lede was also
the thing separating the two near-identical sentences. Now they are adjacent.

**Recommended:** the index heading does a different job, or goes.

| Option | Copy | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | "Where would you start?" | Sets up a list of six. Conversational, and it is the actual question the rows answer |
| B | *(cut the heading entirely)* | The rows are self-evident. Saves 7 words, but leaves the band without a head |
| C | "Five ways in, and one that is all of them." | Accurate and a bit more characterful, but longer |

## 2. Advisory is missing its Payoff

The page's spine is a four-beat structure, and four of the five offers keep it:

| Offer | Need it when | What you get | Payoff | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Fan Engine | ✅ | ✅ (as the four phases) | ✅ | ✅ |
| Sentiment SOS | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Fan Programs | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Fan Moments | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Advisory** | ✅ | ✅ | **missing** | ✅ |

Advisory is also the cheapest, easiest yes on the page, so it is the one where a
reader is closest to acting and the page stops short.

**Recommended:**

> **Payoff:** you get the call right the first time, without carrying a
> full-time hire to do it.

## 3. The same three things have two sets of names

The hero promises three verbs. The index and the section kickers use different
ones for the same three offers:

| | Hero lede says | Index row and section kicker say |
|---|---|---|
| Sentiment SOS | "**Protect** it" | Protect ✅ |
| Fan Programs | "**grow off** it" | **Acquire** ✗ |
| Fan Moments | "**deepen** it" | Deepen ✅ |

Two of three match. The third does not, and "acquire" is the odd word in
another way too: it is the only piece of marketing jargon on a page that
otherwise says "your community has turned on you in public".

**Recommended:** use **Grow** in both places, and fix the clumsy "grow off it"
in the lede at the same time.

> **Lede:** "Make the userbase you already paid for worth more. Protect it, grow
> from it, deepen it, or build the whole system that does all three."
> **Index row:** "Grow. Growth you don't pay for every time."
> **Section kicker:** "Grow"

## 4. Fan Moments is the only offer that names a want, not a problem

Every "Need it when" on the page names something going wrong:

> your growth stops the day you stop paying
> your community has turned on you in public, and it's getting worse
> you pay for every new customer
> you have one decision to get right

Except this one:

> **Fan Moments:** "you want to give your best fans a moment they'll love."

It is circular (you need a fan moment when you want a fan moment) and it is the
only one that does not give the reader a reason to act now. It is also the
offer where the proof is strongest, so it deserves better.

**Recommended:**

> **Need it when:** your best customers get exactly what everyone else gets, and
> nothing you do makes them feel any different.

## 5. "Plug-and-play" promises something the page then contradicts

> "Each one plug-and-play. If you don't have the team to run it, I bring one."

Two lines later the Fan Engine is "6 to 8 weeks to build, then ongoing", and
the second half of this very sentence says you may not have the team to run it.
Plug-and-play means neither. It is also the one piece of tech-cliché on the
page.

**Recommended:** "Each one stands on its own. If you don't have the team to run
it, I bring one."

## 6. Sentiment SOS says its timeline twice and its urgency twice

Within about 80 words:

> **What you get:** ... a fix shipped across product and community **in one to
> two weeks** ...
> **Duration line:** "**1 to 2 weeks** · Faster if it can't wait"
> **Under the CTA:** "In a crisis right now? We can start this week."

The duration line is the one to thin, because the other two are doing more:
"What you get" earns the timeline in context, and the CTA note is the only one
that says something concrete about *starting*.

**Recommended:** duration line becomes just "1 to 2 weeks". Drop "Faster if it
can't wait", which the CTA note says better.

## 7. Meta title is 65 characters (pre-existing)

Counted, not eyeballed:

| Title | Chars | |
|---|---|---|
| "Work with me · Laura Cordrey · Fan-led growth for consumer brands" | **65** | current, over |
| **"Work with me · Laura Cordrey · Fan-led growth"** | **45** | recommended |
| "Work with me · Laura Cordrey" | 28 | shortest |

The description is 146 and fine. This predates today's work, and the handover
says leave the over-long case-study titles alone, but that exemption was for
`Client · Result · Laura Cordrey` patterns where the client name survives
truncation. This one just runs long.

## 8. Not a problem: the index rows repeat the Payoff lines

Worth naming so it does not get "fixed" later. Three index rows are close to
verbatim the Payoff of the section they point at:

> Index: "Deepen. Your top customers spend more and stay longer."
> Section Payoff: "your top customers spend more and stay longer."

This is the skim layer promising something and the section delivering the same
promise. A reader who skims gets the payoff; a reader who reads gets it
confirmed where they decide. Leave it.
