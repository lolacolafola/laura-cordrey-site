# Proposal: entry copy for the Fan Score™ (and both tool meta descriptions)

**Date:** 23 July 2026
**Status:** Options for Laura to pick from. Nothing applied.
**Supersedes:** nothing. This is the first proposal for the tool entry copy.
It acts on the diagnosis in `review-tools-entry-copy-23jul.md` (findings 2.1, 2.5).

**Already applied separately** (tranche 1, the consistency fixes with one right
answer): the Fan Value H1 is now "What are your fans worth?", the cross-link
punctuation is fixed, and the missing ™ marks are in. Those needed no decision.
This file covers the part that does.

---

## The one thing being fixed

The Fan Score intro screen never says what the tool is about. Reading order today
is: trademark, category, category, mechanics, trademark. The word *customer*
appears zero times before the CTA. The stake, that the people you already paid
for are worth more than you are getting, is the whole reason to spend two
minutes, and it is not on the screen.

Every option below puts the stake in slot one and lets "fan-led" be the name of
the answer rather than the question.

**Assumption, flagged.** All options use *customers*, not *users* or *userbase*.
That is the homepage's own noun and it fits consumer brands, which is who the
homepage says this is for. If the tools are deliberately aimed at product
companies, say so and I will rebuild these around *users*, but the two pages
should not use different words for the same people.

---

## Option A: the money already spent

Closest to the homepage, and the safest. It leads with a sunk cost the reader
already feels.

| Slot | Copy |
|---|---|
| Eyebrow | The Fan Score™ |
| H1 | **You already paid for these customers.** |
| Lede | You paid to win them once. Fans stay, spend more, and bring you new customers, so every one you turn into a fan is growth you do not pay for twice. |
| Get-line | *unchanged:* A few honest questions. Two minutes to your result and the one move to grow it. |
| CTA | *unchanged:* Get my Fan Score™ |

**Why it works.** It opens on a fact about the reader rather than a claim about
a category, and "growth you do not pay for twice" carries the paid-versus-fan
argument without needing the word *paid growth*.

**Risk.** The H1 is a statement, not a question, so it does less to signal that
a diagnostic follows. The get-line has to do that work alone.

---

## Option B: the question they can answer on instinct

The strongest opening if you want the headline to pull, and it directly fixes
finding 2.1 (a headline the visitor cannot hold an opinion about).

| Slot | Copy |
|---|---|
| Eyebrow | The Fan Score™ |
| H1 | **How many of your customers would recommend you?** |
| Lede | That is the difference between a customer and a fan. Fans stay, spend more, and bring you new customers, and most brands have more of them than they think, or far fewer. |
| Get-line | *unchanged:* A few honest questions. Two minutes to your result and the one move to grow it. |
| CTA | *unchanged:* Get my Fan Score™ |

**Why it works.** Everyone has an instinct about this and nobody has the number,
which is the exact gap a two-minute diagnostic fills. The Fan Score then arrives
as the name for the thing they just wanted to know.

**Risk.** "Would recommend" is close enough to NPS that a marketing reader may
assume that is all this measures. If the quiz covers more than advocacy, and it
does, the H1 slightly undersells it. Worth checking against the question set
before choosing this one.

---

## Option C: minimal change, keep the category

If you would rather not move far. One noun swap does most of the work.

| Slot | Copy |
|---|---|
| Eyebrow | The Fan Score™ |
| H1 | **How many of your customers are fans?** |
| Lede | Fans stay, spend more, and bring you new customers. This tells you how many you have today, and the one move that would grow the number. |
| Get-line | *unchanged* |
| CTA | *unchanged* |

**Why it works.** It keeps the shape and rhythm of the current H1 while replacing
the abstraction ("your growth") with people ("your customers"). Lowest risk of
knock-on effects anywhere else.

**Risk.** Still a question the visitor cannot answer, so it fixes the positioning
but not the pull.

---

## A note on the line you would be dropping

"Paid growth stops the moment you stop paying. Fan-led growth keeps going."

It is a good line and it is a real argument. Two reasons it is not in any option
above as the lede: it is the fourth thing a cold visitor needs, not the second,
and it defines the offer against another channel rather than against the
reader's situation, which sits close to the compare-down framing you have ruled
out elsewhere. **It would work well further down the page, or on the result
screen where the reader has a number and is deciding what to do about it.**
Worth keeping somewhere rather than losing.

## Meta descriptions

These are what appear in search, for people who have never heard the term.
Counted, not estimated. Titles capped at 60, descriptions at 160.

### Fan Score

| | Copy | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Current | A short diagnostic that tells you how fan-led your growth is today, and the one move that would grow it. Two minutes. | 117 |
| **A** | The customers you already paid for are worth more than you are getting. Two minutes to see how much more, and the one move that gets it. | 136 |
| **B** | Find out how many of your customers would stay, spend more and recommend you, and the one move that would grow the number. Two minutes. | 135 |
| **C** | A two-minute diagnostic: how many of your customers are really fans, what that gap is costing you, and the one move that closes it. | 131 |

Pair the description with whichever H1 option you take. A goes with option A,
B with B, C with C.

### Fan Score title

The current title is **exactly 60 characters**, at the ceiling, so any longer
question forces a trade.

| | Copy | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Current | The Fan Score™ · How fan-led is your growth? · Laura Cordrey | 60 |
| **T1** | The Fan Score™ · Are your customers fans? · Laura Cordrey | 57 |
| **T2** | The Fan Score™ · How many of your customers are fans? | 53 |

T2 fits the full question but drops "Laura Cordrey". Worth it only if you think
the question earns more clicks than the name does. On a page most people reach
from the site rather than from search, T1 is probably the safer call.

### Fan Value Model

The title here already says "What your fans are worth", so it now matches the
new H1 with no change needed. Only the description leads with the category.

| | Copy | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Current | A quick estimate of what fan-led growth is worth to your brand each year, built from published benchmarks. Conservative, and it shows its work. | 143 |
| **A** | Put in a few numbers you already have and see what your fans are worth: what you gain when they stay, spend more, and bring you new customers. | 142 |
| **B** | A conservative estimate of what your customers are worth once they become fans, built from published benchmarks. It shows its work. | 131 |

A leads with the reader's own numbers and mirrors the homepage promise. B keeps
the credibility framing ("published benchmarks", "shows its work") that makes
this tool trustworthy, which matters more here than on the Fan Score, since this
one outputs money figures. **B is my recommendation for this page specifically.**

---

## My recommendation

**Option B for the Fan Score H1, description B, title T1.** It is the only option
that fixes both problems at once: it leads with the customer rather than the
category, and it asks something the reader actually wants answered. Take option C
instead if you want the smallest possible change, and A if you want the money
argument foregrounded over the curiosity one.

**Fan Value description B**, as above.

Check before B is applied: that the quiz measures more than advocacy, and that
the H1 is not promising a narrower thing than the tool delivers.

---

## Claims needing confirmation

None of the copy above asserts a new factual or performance claim. "Most brands
have more of them than they think, or far fewer" in option B's lede is the only
line making a claim about the world. It is deliberately unfalsifiable in its
current form, but if you would rather not gesture at data you do not have, cut
the clause and the lede still stands.
