# Does "fan-led growth" have search demand?

Date: 22 Jul 2026
Scope: focused keyword-demand check to settle the "own the IP or own the sector"
decision. Not a site audit.

**Method caveat, stated up front.** No Ahrefs or Semrush connector is
authorised on this session, so there are **no volume numbers here.** What
follows is inferred from SERP composition and competitive density — what
actually ranks for a term, and how many companies have built dedicated pages
for it. That is legitimate evidence and it is decisive at this end of the scale
(a term with real commercial demand always has a glossary-page ecosystem), but
it is not the same as volume data. Confirm with a keyword tool before spending
real money on it.

---

## Finding 1 — "fan-led growth" has effectively no commercial search demand

An unqualified search for the exact phrase returns, in order: LED ceiling fan
market reports, the UK's football **fan-led review**, LED grow lights with
circulation fans, **lauracordrey.com**, and more grow-light hardware.

Two things follow:

- **The term is homonym-poisoned.** "Fan" means an electrical appliance to the
  entire commercial web, and "fan-led review" is an established UK football
  governance story with news-grade authority. Laura would be competing forever
  against two unrelated, much higher-volume meanings. That handicap does not go
  away with better content.
- Adding marketing qualifiers (`"fan-led growth" marketing brand strategy
  consultant -football -LED`) returns **nothing using the term** — just generic
  brand-strategy consultancies. Nobody else in marketing uses this phrase.

## Finding 2 — she already ranks page one for it, and it is worth almost nothing

lauracordrey.com appears on page one of the unqualified exact-match search,
alongside Home Depot and Sky Sports. She owns the term. She owns it because
**nobody is competing for it.**

Owning an uncontested term is worth roughly zero traffic. It does mean the
category-creation slot is genuinely open, which is a real if long-dated asset.

## Finding 3 — the adjacent categories are mature and busy

**"Community-led growth"** has a full definition-page ecosystem: PartnerStack,
Mighty Networks, Teachfloor, Chameleon, NoGood, Bettermode, Zelos, BuddyBoss,
Zapnito. Nine companies with dedicated glossary entries is a reliable proxy for
real, monetisable volume — nobody writes an encyclopedia page for a term with
no traffic.

**"Brand advocacy" / "advocacy marketing"** likewise: Mentionlytics, Duel,
Slate, Social Firm and several 2026 "state of" guides.

**"Superfan"** is live and commercially contested, with agencies already
positioned on it (Superfan Brands, altterrain). It is also the closest existing
term to Laura's actual positioning.

## Finding 4 — the demand is problem-shaped, not category-shaped

"How to turn customers into fans" returns Forbes (twice), Tony Robbins,
Brittany Hodak's *Superfans*, David Meerman Scott's *Fanocracy*, Pragmatic
Institute, and several agencies.

**That is a crowded, high-demand query set.** People are absolutely searching
for what Laura does. They are just not using her label to do it.

---

## Recommendation

**This changes my earlier advice, and the reason matters.**

I argued the other day for "sector in front, IP behind", on the grounds that
search only works for the category. That reasoning assumed the category had
demand. It does not. So the argument loses its support — but it does **not**
flip to IP-first, because "the Fan Engine" has even less demand than "fan-led
growth".

**Lead with the problem. Keep both names as assets, not as keywords.**

| Layer | What it is | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **The problem language** | "turn customers into fans", "grow without paid ads", "advocacy that pays back" | **Getting found.** This is where the demand is. |
| **Fan-led growth** | the category label | **Getting remembered.** A stage line and a brand, not a keyword. |
| **The Fan Engine™** | the IP | **Getting paid.** A closing asset, downstream of interest. |

Three concrete consequences:

1. **Stop treating "fan-led growth" as an SEO target.** Keep it everywhere as
   branding — it is distinctive, it is hers, and it is excellent when said out
   loud on a stage. Just stop expecting it to bring traffic.

2. **Retarget the `/fan-led-growth` page at the question people actually ask.**
   Its title and `h1` currently answer *"What is fan-led growth?"* — a query
   with no searchers. The page should answer *"How do you turn customers into
   fans?"* and treat "fan-led growth" as the **answer**, not the question. That
   is a title, `h1` and `FAQPage` change, not a rewrite: the body copy already
   answers the right question.

3. **The merge still holds.** One page, not two. If anything this strengthens
   it: two pages splitting authority is worse when the term is already fighting
   ceiling fans and football for the SERP.

### Riskiest assumption

That SERP composition is a good enough proxy for volume. It is directionally
reliable but it cannot tell you whether "how to turn customers into fans" is
1,000 searches a month or 100. Before committing budget, confirm in a keyword
tool. It would not change the ranking of the three options above, only the size
of the prize.

### Worth a separate think

**"Superfan" may be the bridge term.** It has real demand, it is far closer to
Laura's positioning than "community-led growth", and it does not collide with
appliances or football. Using it as connective language — not as a rename —
could carry the demand without giving up the brand. Parked, not recommended
yet.
